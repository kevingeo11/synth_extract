Polymer Scope Definition

INCLUDE only if **all four gates pass**.

Gate 1 — Material creation
The authors either **(a)** formed or altered covalent bonds on a polymer backbone — polymerization, functionalization, grafting, crosslinking, chain extension, or derivatization of a purchased or natural polymer — or **(b)** created a new multi-component material by formulation (blending, filling, plasticizing). Blending two purchased grades qualifies under (b).

"New material" means ≥2 identified components combined, or one chemically altered; solvent is not a component. Purchased polymers are fine **only if** supplier, grade, or product code is stated.

**OUT:** an acquired polymer with no new composition, even when physically transformed — dissolved, cast, annealed, drawn, self-assembled, or gelled by a trigger. Also out: unidentifiable polymer source.

Gate 2 — Material-level property
At least one measured property **of the material itself**: mechanical, thermal, rheological, transport (conductivity, permeability, diffusivity), optical, surface, sorption, swelling, network, degradation, morphological, or *in vitro* biological on the material.

**OUT if the only properties are device- or system-level:** power conversion efficiency, *J*sc/*V*oc/FF, EQE, transistor mobility, battery capacity or cycle life, sensor sensitivity or LOD, actuator stroke, module flux or rejection, LED luminance, capacitance, reactor turnover, *in vivo* outcomes.

**This applies even when the material was synthesized in-work.** If both levels are reported, INCLUDE based on the material-level properties.

Gate 3 — Active polymeric role
The polymer must drive the measured property.

**OUT** when it is a binder, inert support or substrate, sacrificial template or porogen, or processing aid and the measured property belongs to another component.

**Test:** if the polymer could be swapped for any comparable material without changing the conclusion, it is passive.

Role is judged **per property, not per component**: a binder is passive for the filler's gas adsorption but active for the composite's mechanics.

Gate 4 — Tier and form
**IN:** monomer synthesis feeding an in-work polymerization, polymerization, post-polymerization chemistry, formulation.

**OUT:** shaping (extrusion, molding, spinning, drawing, foaming, calendering); CAD-designed molds, tooling, mold-filling and cavity studies; printing judged on fidelity, resolution, or geometry; article performance (scaffolds, separators, adhesive joints, gaskets); devices and systems.

A printable formulation whose printed *material* properties are measured is IN.

**Thin films IN, coatings OUT.**

Discriminator: is the film the material under study, or a layer applied to modify a substrate?

Film properties measured (modulus, *T*g, conductivity, permeability, morphology, domain spacing, membrane-coupon transport) → IN, including spin-coated and cast films.

Substrate performance measured (salt spray, corrosion of coated metal, gloss, anti-fouling, hardcoat on the article) → OUT; likewise a film merely carrying another active phase, which fails Gate 3.

If both are reported, INCLUDE based on the film properties.

Hard exclusions (override all gates)

- **Computation only** — MD, DFT, coarse-grained or mesoscale simulation, Monte Carlo, FEA, CFD, QSPR/QSAR, ML property prediction, generative or inverse design, virtual screening. Out even when the modelled material is a real polymer. Experiment **plus** supporting computation is IN on the experimental content.

- **Sequence-defined biological macromolecules** — nucleic acids and their analogues, functional proteins, peptides, and conjugates in which the biomolecule is the functional entity. Out even if crosslinked in-work with material properties measured, because function derives from a specified sequence rather than statistical architecture. Where such a biomolecule is only a minor component of a material built from an in-scope polymer, INCLUDE on that polymer.

  **Biologically derived polymers used as materials remain potentially IN** — polysaccharides, lignin, natural rubber, PLA, PHA, bulk structural proteins, and synthetic polypeptides — provided all four gates pass.

- **Curing, vulcanization, reactive extrusion**, and depolymerization or chemical recycling to monomer. Degradation measured as a material property is IN.

- **Reviews**, perspectives, editorials, book chapters, meta-analyses.

Composites

Formation route does not gate — *ex situ* compounding is admissible; **Gate 3 does the work.** Bonding class, dispersion state, and length scale are attributes, never gates.

The reinforcement may itself be a modifiable polymer, so bond-forming chemistry can occur on the filler backbone; alkali and silane fiber treatments are not established bond events.

Terminology cautions

These carry no scope information; classify by the gates, not the word.

**Resin** — a monomer or oligomer (epoxy, phenolic), a commercial polymer, or a crosslinked network.

**Gel / hydrogel** — a state, possibly non-polymeric.

**Biopolymer** — either sequence-defined (out) or a bulk biologically derived material (potentially in).

Also ambiguous: **composite**, **nanocomposite** (a scale claim), **biocomposite** (feedstock origin), **plastic**, **film**, **coating**, **microgel**, **functional polymer**.

Do not treat "synthesized" in an abstract as evidence of Gate 1; authors may use it for mixing and casting.

Decision rule

Return `true` only when all four gates pass and no hard exclusion applies.

Return `false` if any gate fails, a hard exclusion applies, or the full text does not provide sufficient evidence to establish inclusion.

Format Instructions:

Return only a JSON object with exactly this structure:

{"label": true}

where `label` must be a JSON boolean (`true` or `false`).

Do not include the deciding gate, evidence, explanations, Markdown, code fences, or any additional fields in the final response.
